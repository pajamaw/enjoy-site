Privacy Policy — Enjoy

Last updated: October 10, 2026

Who holds your data

Enjoy is an app for a server that someone runs. Who holds your data depends on which server you signed in to:

(a) You use your own server. You typed the server address yourself (a home machine, a VPS, or any host you control). You hold your data. The server stores your saved items, ratings, reviews, tags, and photos. The developer of Enjoy receives none of it and has no access to it.

(b) You joined a shared server with an invite code. The person who runs that server holds your data: your account email, display name, saved items, photos, ratings, and reviews. That person can see it and can delete it. The developer of Enjoy (Peter Wickwire) runs one such shared server and gives out invite codes for it; if your code came from the developer, the developer is the operator and holds that data. It is used only to provide the service to you. It is not sold, shared, or used for advertising or tracking.

Groups

You can create a group or join one with a join code, and share individual items with it.

  • If you share an item with a group, every member of that group can see it: title, link or address, image, details, tags, your rating and your review. They also see your display name.
  • Members can save their own copy, without your rating or review. They cannot change yours.
  • Unsharing an item, leaving the group, or deleting the group stops the sharing. Copies members already saved stay with them.
  • Anyone who has a group's join code can join until the group's owner makes a new code.
  • Group data lives on the Enjoy server you use. The phone keeps your group list and the most recent page of each group's shared items so they can be shown offline, and removes both with the rest of your data.

Places

Places are saved items like any other. The app does not read your location. If you save or share a maps link, the link is sent to your server, which reads the name and coordinates from it. If the person who runs the server has turned on address lookup, the server sends the coordinates (nothing about you) to a geocoding service to fill in the city, region, and country. On the shared server run by the developer this lookup is on and uses OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service.

The Map view in Places draws your places on a map made from OpenStreetMap data and served by OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org). That service then learns which area of the map the phone is displaying, as with any map; Enjoy does not send it your list of places, and the map does not show or use the phone's own location.

Books and Goodreads

Books are saved items like any other. If you connect a Goodreads profile, you give your Enjoy server your Goodreads profile link (or your private Goodreads feed link). The server then reads your Goodreads shelves every few hours and downloads the cover pictures Goodreads lists for them. It only reads: Enjoy never writes to Goodreads. The link is stored on the server until you disconnect or delete your account. The phone itself contacts Goodreads only when you tap "Open in Goodreads" or "Find on Goodreads". If the person who runs the server has turned on book lookup, the server sends a book's ISBN, or its title and author (nothing about you), to Open Library to fill in missing details.

What is stored on the phone

Enjoy stores the following on your device so it can work offline:

  • Server URL and bearer token — saved in the device's secure store (expo-secure-store). Removed on sign-out, "Use a different server", and account deletion.
  • Reader preferences — text size, theme, and font — stored in the secure store.
  • Share-extension session copy — a copy of the server URL and token written to the App Group container (group.com.pjland.enjoy) so the "Save to Enjoy" share extension can save items without opening the app.
  • Offline save queue — if you share a link while offline, the save is stored locally in the App Group container and sent when the network is available again.
  • Local database — a mirror of your server data (items, tags, saved article text, sync position, cached user info, and the outbox of changes not yet sent) in a SQLite file. Also includes item images downloaded from the server. Removed when you sign in as a different account, choose "Sign out, remove data", or delete your account.
  • Item images — photos taken or picked for beer, wine and other items, and images downloaded from the server, stored in the app's Documents directory.

None of this is sent anywhere except the server you signed in to.

What Enjoy sends to your server

When you are signed in, Enjoy sends the following to the server you signed in to (your own, or the shared one):

  • Your authentication token (to prove your identity).
  • Items you save (URLs, text, photos).
  • Ratings, reviews, and tasting notes you add.
  • Tags you create.
  • Your profile information (name, email) as managed by the server.
  • Search runs on the phone's own copy; search text is not sent anywhere.

All communication is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). The app also supports local-network connections (HTTP on your home network) for self-hosted setups.

Optional AI features

If the server owner enables AI categorization, item content (such as a URL's text or a photo) may be sent to an AI model for automatic tagging. This happens on the server side — the app does not directly contact any AI provider. The model is the one chosen by the person who runs the server (for example, a local Ollama model or the Anthropic API). The app does not control or configure AI features; this depends entirely on the server owner's setup.

What Enjoy does not do

  • No analytics. Enjoy does not send usage data, crash reports, or telemetry to any third party.
  • No ads. Enjoy contains no advertising and does not participate in any ad networks.
  • No tracking. Enjoy does not track you across apps or websites. It does not use any tracking SDKs.
  • No data sharing. Your data goes only to the server you signed in to. It is not sold or passed to any third party.

Deleting your account

In the app, go to Settings > Delete account. This screen says that the account and everything in it (links, beer and wine, ratings, reviews, tags, saved articles, photos) is permanently deleted from the server and from the phone, asks for your password, and has a destructive "Delete my account permanently" button.

On success, the app removes every item, tag, saved article, sync position, cached user and the outbox from the local database; deletes all downloaded and not-yet-uploaded image files; deletes the stored server address and bearer token; deletes the session copy shared with the share extension; deletes any saves waiting in the extension's offline queue; and shows the first-run screen. Reader preferences (text size, theme, font) are kept because they hold no account data.

If the server cannot be reached, the screen says so and nothing changes on the server or the phone; the request is not queued.
